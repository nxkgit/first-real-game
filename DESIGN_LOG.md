# Design Log

A chronological journal of gameplay/design reasoning over time — what was decided, why, and what the thinking was in the moment. Purpose: let the user retrace their own thought process later and reconsider if a past decision turns out to be a wrong turn.

**Rules for this document:**
- Append new entries at the end, under the current session's heading. Add a new `## Session N — YYYY-MM-DD` heading when starting a new day/session of design discussion.
- Never rewrite or delete a past entry, even one that's been superseded — if a decision changes, add a *new* entry noting the change and referencing the old one. The history is the point.
- Entries are high-level reasoning, not a changelog of `implementationplan.md` — that document reflects current state; this one reflects how we got here.

---

## Session 1 — 2026-10-06

**Stack: TypeScript + Phaser over Godot/Unity.**
Decided based on this project's actual purpose — a Claude Code workflow exercise — rather than generic "best engine for beginners" advice. A browser/TS game is 100% text with no GUI-editor-only state, and Claude Code's browser automation tools can click/test/screenshot the running game directly, which isn't possible with a native Godot editor window. Godot was a strong runner-up on general merits (free, text-based scene files, well-regarded for solo devs) but loses specifically on this project's AI-pair-programming fit.

**Genre: turn-based Slay-the-Spire-style deckbuilder roguelike over a real-time arena survivors-like or grid roguelike.**
Turn-based was chosen partly to avoid the real-time "game feel" tuning problem (hit feedback, timing, juice) that's hard to get right and easy for AI-assisted first-pass code to get wrong. Deckbuilder depth-to-build-effort ratio was judged strong for a portfolio piece.

**Magic schools as Hearthstone-Battlegrounds-style "tribes" — explored, then set aside for now.**
Original idea: cards are spells grouped into schools (fire/frost/arcane/nature) with within-school synergy, borrowing BG's tribe-synergy pattern. Raised an open design question: should higher difficulty force players to diversify across schools to survive, or should mono-school be a fully viable committed choice every run?

Reasoning landed on: don't force diversification. StS itself never mixes colors (single color + colorless per character) and is highly replayable via multiple distinct archetypes *within* one color — that's the model to borrow, not forced cross-school mixing. The Battlegrounds comparison also points this way: BG rewards narrowing into 1-2 tribes, not spreading thin, so "tribe synergy" as a concept supports specialization, not forced breadth. Forcing a player out of their chosen school to survive was judged to actively undermine class-fantasy identity, which is a core part of the appeal.

**Refinement: the real tension is in-the-moment adapt-vs-commit, within a hero's own pool, not across schools.**
User clarified they still want meaningful pressure at higher difficulty to choose "what makes me strong right now" vs. "the build I want to commit to" — but located within a single hero's class fantasy, matching how StS's card-reward screens actually create tension (take the on-plan synergy card vs. the generically strong card vs. the card that answers your current weakness), not by requiring a school switch. This was agreed as a refinement consistent with the above, not a contradiction. Mechanisms identified to support this: a real power curve across card rarity, rare build-defining cards that can reshape a run's plan mid-climb, specific tough/elite encounters tuned to punish narrow strategies, and a small neutral/"colorless"-style card pool as an emergency answer valve that doesn't require full cross-school commitment.

**Scope cut: single hero ("Mage") for the initial build; multi-hero roster and multi-school complexity deferred.**
Decided not to get hung up on designing multiple magic schools or the full hero roster (mage/ranger/fighter, etc.) before proving out one hero's core loop. Hero design/characteristics — and all creative/aesthetic/narrative content generally — will be authored by the user, not generated by Claude Code; Claude Code's role is systems/mechanics/architecture.

**Reward structure: card-vs-gold choice at the post-combat reward screen.**
Concern raised: a full Battlegrounds-style shop/economy layer might be too much for players who just want a straightforward loot-and-battle experience. Resolution: offer a choice at the reward screen between a random card or a gold payout (banked for later shop spending), rather than a dedicated shop phase every round. This turns StS's existing-but-mostly-invisible "skip reward" option into a legible choice, lets simple players ignore the economy entirely by always taking the card, and lets economy-minded players opt in — without requiring anyone to learn a parallel system. Noted risk to watch later: gold's value must be tuned so it's a genuine toss-up against the card option, not a dominant or dominated strategy, once shop contents are designed.

**MVP staging locked: MVP 1 = single combat only; MVP 2 = the fuller loop.**
MVP 1: one hero, one enemy, no shop, no relics, no non-combat events — just prove the core draw/play/end-turn combat loop feels right, with placeholder visuals (final art/visual identity is the user's to design). MVP 2: chained combats plus non-combat events (shop included or not, TBD), deployed somewhere shareable for friend playtesting. Everything beyond MVP 1 is explicitly still open and expected to change.

**MVP 1 systems decided:** static energy at 4/turn, draw 5 cards/turn, StS-style draw/discard/reshuffle (draw pile → hand → discard pile, reshuffle discard into draw pile when empty). Enemy intent telegraphing to closely mirror StS (visible icon for the enemy's next move). One life per run (permadeath) planned for the eventual full run, no meta progression for now. Elites and a boss confirmed to exist at the end of the eventual full run (not relevant to MVP 1 itself).

**Reused StS's Attack/Skill/Power card-type split and general status-effect approach (Block, etc.) directly, rather than inventing a different taxonomy.**
Question raised: is this too derivative of StS to simply copy? Reasoning: these are generic, functional game-mechanic primitives, not copyrightable creative expression, and nearly every deckbuilder since StS converges on some version of them because they're well-tested solutions — reusing them is normal craft, not creative theft. Real differentiation should come later from the hero's own signature mechanics (the way StS itself differentiates Ironclad/Silent/Defect/Watcher via unique per-character systems), not from renaming basic taxonomy. Flagged in `implementationplan.md` as a placeholder to revisit once hero-specific identity work happens — worth re-reading this entry if that revisit starts to feel like it's being skipped.

**Attack cards are now targeted (drag/click onto the enemy) rather than click-to-play; enemy intent and block readouts switched from words to icons.**
From the first playtest of MVP 1: clicking an attack card and having it fire immediately felt wrong — the user wanted the StS-style act of aiming a card at the enemy. Cards now declare a `target` in their data (`'enemy'` for attacks), and the logic layer rejects playing an enemy-targeted card without a target, so the rule lives in the game rules rather than only in the UI. Skills and powers still play on click. With a single enemy the choice of target is trivial for now, but the interaction is the point, and it's the shape needed once multi-enemy fights arrive. Separately, the "Attack 8" / "Defend 6" / "Block 6" text above and below the enemy read as janky; replaced with a sword or shield icon plus the number, matching the player's existing shield readout. Overall verdict from that playtest otherwise: the core loop "seems fine."

**MVP 1 called done; MVP 2 scope set for the first build: polish, then a short linear run with a card-or-gold reward and a rest stop.**
After the first playtest the user judged the core loop good enough to move on. Before building MVP 2, scope was settled up front (so a long stretch of building could happen without stopping to ask): (1) first finish playtest-friendly polish — a game shared by link has to work at any window size and on phones, and icon-only readouts need hover explanations; (2) keep all MVP 1 open-question numbers (enemy stats, starter deck, Block-only statuses) as provisional placeholders rather than block on them; (3) after each won fight, offer the card-or-gold choice from the earlier reward-structure entry, with gold banked even though there's no shop yet; (4) the only non-combat stop is a rest site that heals. Reasoning for the last two: they're the smallest versions that make fights *chain* in a meaningful way — HP now matters across fights (so rest has a purpose) and the deck grows (so rewards have a purpose) — without needing any narrative/event content, which is the user's to write. Banking gold before the shop exists is deliberate: it lets playtesters already face the card-vs-gold choice, even though gold's actual value can't be tuned until there's something to buy. Risk noted: until the shop lands, taking gold is strictly worse than any card, so playtest feedback on that choice won't mean much yet.

**Sound direction: "fantasy vibe" (user's call); placeholder audio rebuilt to match. Turn order on screen confirmed as discard → enemy acts → draw.**
The user asked for the sound effects to feel more fantasy. Since audio is aesthetic (the user's lane), the replacement set is still explicitly placeholder: generated bells, harp-like plucks, blade whooshes, and shield clangs in one key with a hall reverb, so the user can keep, tweak, or replace individual sounds later. Separately, the user flagged that ending a turn looked out of order. The rules already ran discard → enemy turn → draw (matching the StS model in the plan), but the screen showed the next hand arriving before the enemy acted. That was a display bug, fixed so the screen follows the rules; no gameplay rule changed.

## Session 2 — 2026-10-07

**Weak, Vulnerable and Strength added, mirroring Slay the Spire's rules (all numbers provisional).**
User picked these three as the first status effects. Rules chosen, all following the plan's "mirror StS closely": Weak makes the holder's attacks deal 25% less; Vulnerable makes the holder take 50% more attack damage; Strength adds flat damage to each hit and lasts the whole fight. Damage is computed as (base + Strength) × Weak × Vulnerable, rounded down. Weak and Vulnerable are timed: they count down by 1 at the end of each round (after the enemy acts). A status the enemy puts on the player skips that same round's countdown, so "Weak 1" really does last through the player's next turn instead of vanishing at once. The enemy's intent number now includes these modifiers, so Weakening it visibly lowers the number. Statuses work on both sides: cards can apply them to the enemy or to yourself, and an enemy move can apply them to the player or to itself. Placeholder content to exercise them: four reward-pool cards (Weaken, Expose, Sunder, Strengthen), a Weak-on-you move for Enemy B, and a Strength-on-itself move for Enemy C (whose Heavy Attack dropped from 16 to 12 to offset the Strength). The multipliers (0.75 and 1.5) are in tunables.ts.

**Shop: a rough draft screen only, to give a visual idea (not a decision).**
User asked to see a rough shop. Added a ShopScene: four reward-pool cards on a counter at a flat placeholder price (40 gold), red price when unaffordable, SOLD marker, Leave button. A shop stop was inserted into the placeholder run path between fight 2 and the rest stop. Nothing about contents, pricing, or gold's value against a card is decided; all of it is open. Kept on its own branch so it can be dropped.

**Decided: build with several enemies per fight in mind; enemy moves use the same effects as cards; card text is generated.**
In review of the code's structure the user decided: (1) card descriptions are generated from a card's effects, so a number is written once; (2) an enemy move is a list of the same effects cards use (damage, block, apply a status), so one move can do several things, like attack and debuff; (3) the intent readout works like StS: it shows the kind of move (attack with its damage, defend with its block, buff, debuff) but not which status, and the player is not told what a buff or debuff is; (4) fights hold a list of enemies, each with its own HP, block, statuses and move position, and aimed cards pick which one they hit; (5) cards are looked up by id from one registry, and each card says which hero owns it (or 'neutral') and whether it can appear as a reward. Enemies that die drop out of the fight and the fight is won when all are down. Deferred on purpose: the effect registry (until there are many more effect kinds), hero definitions and the once-per-turn hero ability (the user wants each hero to have one, later), rarity, and status cards that enemies put into the player's deck (the user's idea for later). Placeholder content added to exercise this: Enemy D, and fight 2 is now Enemy B plus Enemy D. Number keys play an aimed card at the first living enemy; mouse aiming picks any.

**Tooling for playtests and balance work, built while the core design decisions are deliberated.**
The user wants to stay productive without settling core gameplay yet, so a set of changes was agreed that make no gameplay decision: seeded randomness (replayable runs), save and resume, a stop-by-stop run report playtesters can copy and send, a dev panel behind `?dev`, and a headless balance simulator with a simple bot. Two small judgement calls inside them, both easy to change: a fight in progress is not saved (resuming restarts that fight from the start, with the same shuffle so refreshing cannot reroll it), and the simulator's bot picks reward cards at random, so it only shows relative changes, not how real players fare. First numbers from the simulator, for the record, with the placeholder content: the bot wins about 88-91% of runs, loses only a few HP per fight, and does slightly better taking gold than taking random cards (91% vs 88%). Read that as "the placeholder fights are easy and random card picks dilute a deck", not as a balance finding. The plan's Deferred list is unchanged: the balance pass itself is still deferred.

**One-act demo: the run became a branching map to a boss, with elites, relics, events and card upgrades (all placeholder, numbers provisional).**
The user decided to stop deferring the run's shape and build essentially one Slay the Spire act as a demo, with placeholder data, and approved the deferred scaffolding for it: relics (basic ones such as +1 Strength at fight start and +10 max HP), events, elite and boss fights, a branching map, and card upgrades chosen at rest stops instead of healing, like StS. Keyboard targeting for multi-enemy fights was dropped (mouse is enough), and the user said not to worry about git flow for now. Provisional calls made to build it, all easy to change in `src/data/`: the map is 12 floors plus the boss, 5 columns, 4 climbs, with StS-like rules (floor 1 all fights, the floor before the boss all rests, elites and rests from floor 5, shops from floor 4, events from floor 2, no repeats of elite/rest/shop along a path); an elite gives 40 gold instead of 25 and always a relic, whichever of card or gold is taken; the boss gives no reward (beating it wins the run); an event fight gives only that event's listed outcomes, not a normal reward; an upgrade is a second registered card (`<id>+`) so decks, saves and tests need nothing special, and a card can be upgraded once; a max HP gain also heals by the same amount; relics can't repeat; event HP loss never kills (floor of 1 HP); the draft shop stays in the act as a stop kind. The save format moved to version 2 (it stores the whole map), so saves from the earlier linear build are discarded. Settings (volume, mute, animation speed, screen shake) are remembered per browser. The simulator learned the act: first numbers with placeholder content are about a third of runs won by the simple bot picking random cards, about 38% always taking gold, about 76% picking cards by trial fights, with the boss the hardest fight. Treat those as relative, not as balance findings.

## Session 3 — 2026-10-07

**Direction: build the card-synergy engine now, with placeholder cards, and set up unattended (cloud) work to build it and the balance process around it.**
The user wants to automate as much non-creative work as possible and to get the balance process (simulations plus documentation) working before real content exists. Their reasoning: balance tooling is only worth building against the kinds of interaction real cards will have, and they know every build will rest on card-to-card synergy as in Slay the Spire (one card multiplying another, effects triggered by other cards being played). So the engine for those interactions is built now, exercised by clearly-placeholder cards that are not in the reward pool (so the live game is unchanged), and the simulator and balance docs are built to work on whatever the card registry contains. This partly lifts the plan's "Full status-effect roster" deferral for the mechanics needed to support synergies; it does not touch the "Magic-school/tribe synergy system" deferral (hero-level identity is still the user's). The user authorised making any gameplay-adjacent calls in these sessions without asking, choosing whatever best fits the repo's documents; each such call is recorded in the session's PR and in this log. Unattended-session rules are in `docs/AUTOMATION.md`; a CI workflow now checks every branch and PR.

**Synergy engine built (branch `auto/synergy-engine`, unattended).** Built the card-to-card machinery: scaling values, Empowered and a status-multiplying effect, triggers on power cards and relics, tags, exhaust, and gainEnergy / loseHp effects. 20 placeholder cards (`src/data/synergyCards.ts`, `inRewardPool: false`) and two relics (registered, not in `RELIC_POOL`) exercise it; the live game is unchanged. Rules and the how-to are in `docs/SYNERGY_ENGINE.md`. Decisions made without asking (all provisional, each reversible):
- Damage order stays add, then multiply, then defender multiplier; scaled values are part of the base.
- Empowered is a status (stacks = attacks doubled), spent by attack cards only, lasts until used (no end-of-turn expiry), and only applies to attack-card damage (skill and trigger damage is not doubled). Multiplier 2 is in tunables.
- Triggered effects target the first living enemy (no random targeting, no aim UI) and are not card plays.
- A power does not trigger on its own play; trigger order is relics, then powers in play order.
- Recursion guard: nested trigger depth capped at `MAX_TRIGGER_DEPTH = 3`, deeper events ignored, rather than forbidding nesting.
- `hpLost` includes enemy hits that get past block, not only self-damage; `loseHp` ignores block and can kill (as StS).
- Once-per-turn resets as each player turn starts.
- Exhaust: exhaustRandom uses the combat's seeded stream (shared with shuffles, so only fights that use it change); a played exhaust card exhausts after its effects, so it never counts itself.
- Placeholder cards are `owner: 'neutral'`; `allDraftableCards()` is the helper for the simulator.
- Effect kinds reached 8, the plan's threshold for an effect registry; not refactored (noted in the PR).
- Tags are not shown on card faces yet, and the exhaust pile has no on-screen display yet (UI follow-ups).

**Orchestrator calls after the first wave of unattended sessions (user: "use your best judgement", only security/computer-health/data questions come back to them).** Five sessions ran (synergy engine, balance process, invariant tests, coverage tests, code audit). The audit and tests surfaced design questions; decided as follows, all provisional and reversible:
- **Powers leave the deck for the rest of the fight once played** (a new `Deck.powerPile`; they never go to the discard pile or come back). Before this, a played power returned to the discard pile, got reshuffled and could be replayed, stacking its effect (a one-card Strength+2 deck reached Strength 8 in 4 turns; Focus's extra draw stacked). Reason: the plan says to mirror StS, where powers stay in play; and stacking powers would make every power card a degenerate engine, which defeats balancing synergies. This changes the live game: the starter Focus is now played once per fight.
- **Hand capped at 10** (`MAX_HAND_SIZE` in tunables). A card drawn into a full hand goes to the discard pile, as in StS. Before, the hand was unbounded and the layout overflowed past 7 cards.
- **The first player turn keeps combat-start block.** A relic like Guard Token ("6 block at combat start") showed its block and then startPlayerTurn wiped it. Found independently by the audit and the coverage session. Now only later turns reset block.
- **Left as is, on purpose:** an enemy re-applying a debuff you already have skips the countdown for the whole stack (audit C4; StS-like enough, low severity); refreshing to retry a lost fight before pressing Continue stays allowed (the run is a learning project, not a competitive one).
- **Process:** because `gh` is not installed anywhere, branches are merged with plain git through an integration branch (`integration/wave1`) that runs the full `npm run verify` before reaching `main`.

**Balance analysis of the synergy cards, and the one change applied from it (orchestrator call, provisional).** The synergy balance session (`docs/balance/synergy-findings-2026-10.md`) searched every deck of up to 5 cards and found exactly one real infinite loop: Prime A (free tagged card) with Tag A Echo (draw 1 per tagged card played). With a scaling payoff an 8-card deck dealt about 480 damage per turn. Applied its proposed fix: **Tag A Echo's trigger is now once per turn.** The loop search afterwards finds no loops; the card's measured value in its tag deck drops about 1.2 HP. Reason for applying only this one: it removes a degenerate case in a placeholder card and is cheap to reverse; the other six proposed tweaks, and the finding that every fight is too easy (a random bot beats both elites 100% with the starter deck), are number-tuning on placeholder content, so they wait for real cards and enemies (the user's). Baseline and sample report regenerated for the change.

**Weak plus Vulnerable looked like a bug; chose live numbers on cards (user, 2026-10-07).** The user reported Vulnerable "not applying" (the enemy showed 3 turns of it but damage did not rise). Cause: their hero was Weak, and the two multiply (x0.75 x1.5 = x1.125, so Strike's 6 stays 6 after rounding down). Not a defect: every damage card (28) amplifies correctly alone, and the rule mirrors StS. The real problem was legibility. Options offered: live numbers on cards, better badge tooltips, or changing the rule to additive. The user chose **only live numbers on cards**; the multiplicative rule stays. Queued with the map-variation request and the "include elites and all synergy cards in the pool" request in HANDOFF.md ("Queued by the user").
